# V1.3.0 Tutorial Script - English YouTube Version

## Hook

If you do a lot of 3D printing, you have probably run into this problem before:

How do you turn a model from this... into this?

Maybe the model you want to print is just too big, and there is no way it is going to fit on the build plate.

Or maybe the model has a lot of fine details, and if you print it as one solid piece, removing supports, sanding, and painting later becomes an absolute workout.

So naturally, you think: "Okay, maybe I should learn how to split models."

But if you do not know 3D modeling, you open Blender in one hand, ZBrush in the other, spend a few hours trying to learn them, and the only thing you master is the art of giving up.

And even if you do know how to model, sometimes you just want a faster way. Traditional modeling software is powerful, no question, but the workflow can be pretty heavy.

That is exactly where CHITUBOX's Free Cut feature is worth trying.

This is the final print result we achieved using Free Cut.

And the best part? The workflow is actually very simple.

Just three steps, and you will know how to use it.

## Step 1: Choose The Cut Position

Open CHITUBOX.

In the top toolbar, go to Prepare, then click Cut.

On the right-side panel, choose Free Cut.

The model we are using here is a temple ruins scene.

Take a quick look at the structure.

You can see that the column bases are mostly independent, while the columns themselves are connected by the entablature above.

With Free Cut, we can separate the four columns from the entablature.

This keeps each part relatively complete, while also giving us a better chance at a cleaner, higher-quality print.

## Step 2: Start Cutting

In the right-side operation panel, click Draw Path, or press the number 1 shortcut key to enter drawing mode.

Now add anchor points to draw the curve.

Keep going until the start point and end point connect, creating a closed surface.

Each time you place a point, the algorithm automatically calculates the path between the two points and draws the curve for you.

If you want to move fast, place the anchor points farther apart so the curve closes more quickly.

If you want more precision, place the anchor points closer together. Shorter segments will give you a smoother transition.

And just like that, we have created a cutting surface.

But what if you are not happy with the cut and want to adjust it?

No problem.

You can select any anchor point on the cutting path and drag it to adjust the cutting surface.

You can also insert or delete anchor points at any time.

As the anchor points change, the cutting surface updates in real time, so it is easy to compare and fine-tune the result.

Once the cutting surface looks right, we can move on to the next step: adding connectors.

## Step 3: Add Connectors

Connectors allow the cut surfaces to lock together without glue, which makes assembly and disassembly much easier.

First, enable Key and Hole.

CHITUBOX includes two built-in connector types: Hexagonal Frustum and Quadrangular Frustum.

You can further adjust the connector settings in the parameter panel.

The first four parameters control the connector height, bottom width, side-to-base angle, and length-width tolerance.

For parts that do not need extreme precision, you can usually adjust these until they look about right.

Tolerance is typically set between 0.1 and 0.3 mm.

Just remember: you also need to consider whether the resin you are using tends to shrink or expand, and adjust the tolerance accordingly.

After setting the parameters, click the Refresh button.

And that is it.

But what if one connector is not enough?

Easy.

You can adjust the number and spacing of the connectors however you like.

Add as many as you need.

If a connector is unnecessary or placed in the wrong spot, you can delete it directly.

## Extra Notes

In most cases, once you reach this point, the cutting setup is basically done.

But some of you may have noticed two buttons: "Flip Cap" and "Flip."

So what do they actually do?

As we know, after a flat surface goes through support placement, resin curing, sanding, and other post-processing steps, it can deform to some degree.

If you use Plane Cut, the two cut surfaces may no longer fit tightly after deformation, which can leave a visible gap.

The surface created by Free Cut is not purely flat.

Instead, one side is slightly concave, and the other side is slightly convex.

This helps the two cut surfaces fit together better, even after the model goes through multiple processing steps.

The "Flip Cap" button controls which side is concave and which side is convex.

"Flip" controls the direction of the connector tenon.

Which side the tenon should be placed on depends on your specific model and assembly needs.

So if you want more detailed control, do not overlook these two buttons.

Now click Apply and check the split result.

Looks pretty good!

Next, we can add supports and finish the rest of the preparation.

Everything is ready. Time to print.

## Ending

One of the best parts of 3D printing is watching something go from a digital model to a real object in your hands.

The hardest part may be handled by the printer itself, but pre-processing and post-processing are still a big part of the whole workflow.

By splitting the model into parts during pre-processing, we get more freedom to adjust the model orientation and support strategy.

That usually means a better print result.

Model splitting also makes post-processing much easier.

Removing supports, sanding, painting...

When you improve just one step, the entire printing workflow benefits.

And the final result?

It gets taken to the next level.
